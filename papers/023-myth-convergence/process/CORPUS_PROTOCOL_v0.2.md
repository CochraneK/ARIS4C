# CORPUS + SOURCE-BUNDLE PROTOCOL · v0.2

## Principle

The corpus is sampled by **tradition-time × motif-family**, not by “find the most famous version of a myth.”

## Source priority

A > primary text / critical edition / scholarly translation with stable locator  
B > specialist secondary source with explicit passage trail  
C > curated comparative database with source linkage  
D > discovery-only tertiary material

D cannot create confirmatory motif codes.

## Bundle freeze

For each case × family:
1. list all candidate canonical witnesses found by the systematic source search;
2. record date, language, edition/translation, completeness, and why included/excluded;
3. freeze a primary bundle before computing expanded similarity;
4. freeze at least one alternate bundle where reasonable;
5. treat lacunae and corpus gaps as missing, not absent.

## Translation sensitivity

Where two defensible translations materially change motif coding:
- preserve source-language locator;
- code both translations in sensitivity analysis;
- do not silently choose the translation that improves reliability or similarity.

## Negative evidence

Tradition-level absence requires a declared coverage argument. Otherwise only bundle-level absence is allowed.

## Expanded ontology

Pilot 24 motifs remain an anchor set.
The 56-motif candidate ontology is re-reviewed before use:
- merge duplicates;
- test sibling exclusivity;
- freeze inclusion/exclusion rules;
- predeclare family hierarchy;
- independently validate new motifs.

## Contamination control

Coders see:
- source text,
- frozen codebook,
- their own packet.

They do not see:
- other coder answers,
- similarity ranks,
- contact hypotheses,
- expected mechanisms.
