# ARIS4C021 · Literature Position v0.1

## Anchor 1 · Harff structural risk model

Barbara Harff (2003), No Lessons Learned from the Holocaust? Assessing Risks of Genocide and Political Mass Murder since 1955, uses a case-control design over political-instability episodes. The published model identifies six major structural risk factors:

- prior genocide/politicide;
- political upheaval;
- exclusionary ideology of the ruling elite;
- autocracy;
- ethnic-minority / ethnic character of the ruling elite;
- low trade openness.

The original question is conditional: among episodes of internal war/regime collapse, which escalate into geno-/politicide?

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055403000522

## Anchor 2 · Williams QCA

Timothy Williams (2016), More Lessons Learned from the Holocaust — Towards a Complexity-Embracing Approach to Why Genocide Occurs, explicitly applies QCA to 139 genocide/non-genocide cases.

The paper reports sufficiency for a configuration centered on:

autocracy AND salient elite ethnicity AND (exclusionary ideology OR political upheaval).

This makes Williams the direct methodological predecessor of ARIS4C021.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.5038/1911-9933.9.3.1306

Open record: https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/gsp/vol9/iss3/11/

## Anchor 3 · Modern early-warning benchmark

The US Holocaust Memorial Museum's Early Warning Project uses publicly available country-level data and statistical/machine-learning models to estimate the risk of new intrastate mass killing. Its operational outcome is deliberately different from legal genocide or Harff geno-/politicide, so it is used as a secondary outcome/predictive benchmark rather than silently merged with the historical replication.

Public methodology reference:
https://www.ushmm.org/genocide-prevention/blog/how-to-use-the-early-warning-projects-statistical-risk-assessment-2024

## Gap addressed by 021

The project does not claim that QCA on genocide is new.

The intended contribution is:
1. exact historical replication;
2. modern data extension;
3. explicit outcome-definition sensitivity;
4. necessity vs sufficiency stability;
5. causal asymmetry / non-onset pathways;
6. leave-one-case and leave-one-region fragility;
7. QCA vs predictive-model comparison;
8. a provenance-linked periodic-table-like condition atlas that exposes uncertainty.

## Interpretation rule

A QCA solution is sample- and calibration-dependent. “Necessary” and “sufficient” are reported with the exact case universe, outcome definition, consistency, coverage and sensitivity results. The manuscript will avoid language implying that a single social condition mechanically causes genocide.
