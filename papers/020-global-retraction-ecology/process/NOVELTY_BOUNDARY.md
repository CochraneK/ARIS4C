# Novelty Boundary · 2026-09-24

The project is intentionally positioned beyond a descriptive “who retracts most?” bibliometric paper.

## Nearby recent work

- **Cheng, Yang & Allen (Scientometrics, 2026)** studied the paper-mill subset of Retraction Watch and reported 10,409 paper-mill retractions through 2024.
- **Mañana-Rodríguez et al. (Scientometrics, 2026)** matched 31,910 RWDB retractions to OpenAlex and estimated retraction rates by open-access model, demonstrating why denominators matter.
- **García-Romero et al. (Scientometrics, 2026)** modeled post-retraction citations in 531 papers across Business, Social Sciences and Technology.
- **Cross-disciplinary medical-journal work (2026)** estimated rates for 878 retractions among 422,827 publications in selected top journals across nine medical disciplines.
- Earlier field-specific studies have examined medicine, computer science and other domains, often using restricted journal/database subsets.

## ARIS4C-020 contribution target

A single reproducible framework spanning the **complete current RWDB primary Retraction population** and combining:

1. publication-denominator normalization across the entire disciplinary space;
2. cohort-aware time-to-retraction modeling;
3. the full multi-label reason ecology and co-occurrence structure;
4. mass-event / paper-mill decomposition;
5. field/country/publisher/institution heterogeneity under explicit exposure denominators;
6. post-retraction citation afterlife with matched controls;
7. versioned source snapshots and sensitivity analyses that separate database/detection dynamics from scientific-behavior claims.

If the integrated paper becomes too broad, the shared canonical dataset may support a paper series, but the data spine and ontology remain one source of truth.
