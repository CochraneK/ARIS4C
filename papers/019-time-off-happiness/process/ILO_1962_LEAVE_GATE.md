## Coverage

The article studies **77 countries** and classifies the representative initial annual paid vacation after the first year of employment into three source-defined groups:

- **12 / 77 (15.6%)** — three weeks or more;
- **47 / 77 (61.0%)** — about two weeks;
- **18 / 77 (23.4%)** — one week or more but less than two weeks. citeturn341677search22turn971350search34

The country-level membership is now fully materialized. Appendix II exact rows for the <2-week group include Argentina 10 days, Burma 7 consecutive days with at least 6 working days, Canada 1 week, Greece 6/8 working days, Guatemala 5/10 days, Honduras 10 working days, Ireland 7 days, Japan 6 days and Luxembourg 8 working days, followed by the remaining entries on the next page. citeturn408537search31turn408537search33

## Three-weeks-or-more group

The article states that Norway and Sweden had 18 working days, Cuba/Nicaragua/Panama 30 days or one month, and seven more countries moved from two to three weeks between 1953 and 1960: Denmark, France, Monaco, Uruguay, Togo, Finland and Malagasy Republic. citeturn341677search22

## Measurement guardrails

The 1962 groups are **source-defined**, not mechanically reconstructed from modern five-day-workweek arithmetic.

Examples:
- Jordan's raw provision is described as three weeks, but public holidays count inside the period; the article places Jordan in the “about two weeks” group.
- Australia, Canada, India, West Germany, Italy, Netherlands, South Africa, Switzerland, UK and USA have subnational, sectoral, statutory-order or collective-bargaining complications.
- Historical state identities such as Byelorussia, Czechoslovakia, GDR/FRG, U.S.S.R., UAR and Yugoslavia are preserved and must not be naively mapped to a current state for longitudinal change estimates.

## Legislative-change evidence during the prior decade

The article reports first-time paid-vacation legislation in Burma, Cambodia, Honduras, Jordan, Libya, Paraguay, Turkey and Viet-Nam, plus new statutory coverage at federal/subnational level in Canada, India and West Germany. It also identifies 15 countries where amendments extended the basic minimum duration. citeturn971350search31

This makes the 1962 source useful not only as a cross-section but also as a candidate generator for 1950s reforms.
