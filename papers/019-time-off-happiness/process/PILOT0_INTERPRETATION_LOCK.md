# ARIS4C019 · Pilot-0 Interpretation Lock v0.1

**Locked:** 2026-09-21  
**Scope:** Life Ladder only  
**Secondary affect outcomes:** remain locked

> This file freezes the substantive interpretation after the original eight-event analysis, the WHR2024 source refresh, the bounded legal-isolation audits, and the independently frozen Israel holdout. Later analyses may refine uncertainty, but must not silently strengthen these claims.

## 1. What the project can now say

### A. The original eight-event panel does not identify a clean annual-leave-specific causal effect

The frozen eight-event panel is useful as a transparent stress test of labour/time-off reforms in which statutory annual leave changed, but most events are bundled with broader labour-law changes, working-time rules, public holidays, jurisdictional limits, or major macro/security shocks.

The original donor-adjusted WHR2023 result was:

- mean full-post gap: **+0.110**
- median: **−0.099**
- event signs: **3 positive / 5 negative**
- leaving Bahrain out: mean becomes **negative**

The WHR2024 annual-source refresh through 2023 produces:

- mean: **+0.088**
- median: **−0.099**
- event signs: **2 positive / 6 negative**
- leaving Bahrain out: **−0.117**

Modern staggered-adoption diagnostics do not rescue a causal interpretation because pretrend diagnostics are not clean.

### B. The bounded historical legal queue does not yield hidden clean events

All 11 previously unregistered World Bank leave discontinuities were legally audited. **0/11** were admitted as new clean annual-leave-specific holdouts.

Reasons include:

- broad labour/employment codes;
- mismatch between database jump year and legal effective year;
- entity/jurisdiction-limited scope;
- no verified legal entitlement change, suggesting coding/unit/profile revisions.

The event-count threshold was not relaxed after seeing outcome results.

### C. A modern two-snapshot screen found one cleaner holdout: Israel

Comparing WORLD 2015/16 and Equal Futures 2026 annual-leave snapshots was used only to discover candidate legal changes. Every candidate required independent legal verification.

Israel's Annual Leave Law Amendment No.15 qualifies as a cleaner leave-specific event:

- direct amendment to the Annual Leave Law;
- one extra day from 2016-07-01;
- a second extra day from 2017-01-01;
- nationwide statutory rule;
- not a comprehensive labour-code replacement.

Its design was frozen before the outcome was inspected.

### D. The frozen Israel holdout does not show a stable aggregate increase

Under the strict 115-country donor pool and the pre-frozen 2015 reference year:

- full-post mean adjusted gap: **+0.002**
- post event-time pattern: **+0.177, −0.265, +0.114, −0.018**
- frozen pretrend flags: **not triggered**
- 120-donor sensitivity: **+0.010**
- donor-median sensitivity: **+0.008**
- leave-one-region-out post means stay near zero.

However, post-outcome reference-year diagnostics are materially sensitive:

- 2013 reference: about **−0.306**
- 2014 reference: about **−0.337**
- 2013–2015 mean baseline: about **−0.219**
- 2012–2015 mean baseline: about **−0.175**

Because 2014 and late 2015 both contain documented security disturbances, no alternative baseline may be promoted after the fact. The frozen 2015 reference remains primary, but the sensitivity means the correct interpretation is **not** “a precisely estimated zero causal effect.”

## 2. Strongest defensible conclusion

> Across the currently reproducible annual Life Ladder data, statutory annual-leave reforms do not produce a robust, directionally stable population-level life-evaluation response. The apparent positive pooled signal in a broader reform panel is fragile to legal treatment isolation, influential events, newer outcome data, and event definition. A cleaner independently frozen Israeli leave-specific reform produces a near-zero last-pre-year estimate but is itself sensitive to reasonable post-hoc baseline diagnostics.

This is a **null/fragility/identification result**, not evidence that vacation or paid leave lacks individual well-being benefits.

## 3. What the project must not claim

Do not claim that:

- “more statutory annual leave makes countries happier”;
- “annual leave has no effect on well-being”;
- the Bahrain estimate is a credible leave-specific effect;
- the eight-event average is a clean causal ATT for annual leave;
- the Israel +0.002 estimate is a precise causal zero;
- WHR2025/2026 three-year ranking data are annual observations;
- statutory entitlement equals leave utilization;
- population Life Ladder is the same estimand as worker stress, job satisfaction, vacation recovery, or affect.

## 4. Why the result is scientifically useful

The project exposes three design problems that are often hidden in cross-national policy-outcome studies:

1. **legal-event contamination** — policy reforms are frequently bundled;
2. **measurement/exposure mismatch** — entitlement applies to workers/subgroups while Life Ladder is population-wide;
3. **annual-outcome/reference sensitivity** — modest legal changes compete with substantial year-to-year national shocks.

The legal-isolation audit, source-refresh decomposition, and pre-frozen independent holdout are therefore part of the contribution, not merely robustness appendices.

## 5. Future confirmatory route

The strongest future annual-leave-specific extension is:

- recover a verifiable annual Life Ladder panel through at least 2024/2025;
- keep Mexico 2023 behind the firewall until >=2 full post-treatment annual observations exist;
- prospectively freeze any additional leave-specific reforms discovered from the 2015/16→2026 legal snapshot comparison;
- prefer worker-level or subgroup outcomes where exposure can be matched to the legally affected population.

Positive and negative affect remain closed for the current Pilot-0 paper. Opening them would answer a different outcome question and cannot repair the primary identification problem.
