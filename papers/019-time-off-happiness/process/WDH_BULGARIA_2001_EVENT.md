# ARIS4C019 · Bulgaria 2001 statutory-leave historical WDH event sensitivity

**Frozen legal event:** Labour Code reform effective 31 Mar 2001; basic paid annual leave floor increased from 14 working days to at least 20 working days.  
**Outcome:** WDH 122F ten-step numeral LifeSatisfaction, transformed to 0–10.  
**Status:** low-frequency historical donor-adjusted sensitivity; not a clean causal estimate.

## Design

Bulgaria's 122F series provides:
- 1990: 4.48
- 1997: two findings, 4.06 and 4.82 → within-year mean **4.44**
- 2003: **3.79**
- later observations from 2006 onward.

To avoid using very distant 1990 as the main reference, the main comparison uses:
- Bulgaria pre: **1997**
- Bulgaria post: **2003**
- donor rule: Central/Eastern European countries with a 2003 122F observation and at least one pre-reform 1995–1999 observation.
- donor pre value: nearest available pre-reform year to 1997; an exact tie (Slovenia 1995/1999) is averaged.

Fixed donor pool:
Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania.

This rule is based on geography/time/measure availability, not coefficient sign.

## Main result

- Bulgaria change: **-0.650**
- donor mean pre: **5.308**
- donor mean post: **5.584**
- donor mean change: **0.277**
- donor-adjusted change: **-0.927 LifeSatisfaction points**

Leave-one-donor-out range:
**-1.041 to -0.777**.

Simple donor-placebo absolute-rank diagnostic:
**0.200**.

## Interpretation lock

> Bulgaria shows a sizeable negative donor-adjusted historical LifeSatisfaction signal across the 1997→2003 window, but the temporal resolution is too coarse and the post-socialist transition environment too confounded to attribute this decline to the 2001 annual-leave reform.

Key limitations:
- only one near-reform pre year and one early post point in this measure family;
- donor pre years differ within 1995–1999;
- Central/Eastern Europe experienced large simultaneous institutional, economic and political changes;
- the 122F finding code changes from the earlier WVS-style series to a later series in 2003 across the region;
- ecological national means are not worker-level outcomes.

This result is retained because the legal event was outcome-blind and the negative signal is real in the reconstructed data, but it **does not overturn** the project's overall no-stable-direction conclusion. Alongside Slovakia (~0) and Great Britain (positive B-tier), it strengthens evidence of cross-event heterogeneity.
