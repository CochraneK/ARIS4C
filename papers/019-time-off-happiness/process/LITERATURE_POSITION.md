# ARIS4C019 · Literature Position v0.2

_Updated after Pilot-0 and modern legal-source audit: 2026-09-21._

## 1. Adjacent evidence is stronger than direct evidence

The nearest causal and longitudinal literature concerns:

- reduced weekly working time;
- employer-level shorter-hours programs;
- individual vacation use and recovery;
- paid vacation availability and worker mental health;
- cross-national work-family policy indices.

Examples already frozen in this project include:

- Lepinteur (2019), *Labour Economics*, DOI: 10.1016/j.labeco.2018.05.010.
- Voglino et al. (2022), *BMJ Open*, DOI: 10.1136/bmjopen-2021-051131.
- the 2026 scoping review of reduced hours with maintained pay, DOI: 10.5271/sjweh.4266.
- vacation/well-being meta-analytic and longitudinal literature, which generally concerns actual vacations or worker-level recovery rather than statutory national entitlement.

A targeted post-Pilot literature search did **not identify a directly analogous global quasi-experimental study of leave-specific statutory annual-leave reforms on annual national Life Ladder**. This is a search finding, not a claim that no such paper exists.

## 2. The institutional literature has strengthened in 2026

The International Labour Organization published **Global legislative trends on paid annual leave** on 27 July 2026, emphasizing annual leave as part of working-time protection, worker health, work-life balance and productivity, and situating national laws relative to Convention No.132.

Equal Futures released an **Annual Leave and Weekly Rest 2026** public-use dataset covering all 193 UN member states as of January 2026, including:

- annual-leave duration;
- tenure requirements;
- weekly rest;
- agricultural-worker coverage;
- domestic-worker coverage.

This gives ARIS4C019 a substantially better legal discovery layer than the older World Bank Employing Workers panel, but the 2026 dataset is a current-law snapshot rather than a ready-made historical event-time panel.

## 3. Updated contribution

The paper's contribution is no longer framed as simply estimating “the effect of more vacation days.”

It is:

> a falsification-first test of whether legally verified statutory annual-leave changes can be credibly linked to national Life Ladder trajectories when legal bundles, outcome-source revisions, donor contamination, staggered timing and reference-year shocks are made explicit.

The empirical architecture has two layers:

1. **broader eight-event stress-test panel** — useful for showing how apparently positive pooled estimates become fragile after legal isolation and modern event-study diagnostics;
2. **independently frozen Israel holdout** — a cleaner leave-specific amendment discovered only after the original analysis and evaluated under a precommitted donor rule.

## 4. Why a weak/unstable aggregate effect is theoretically coherent

A statutory entitlement reform may have a small national Life Ladder effect even if actual vacations benefit workers because:

- only a subset of residents are employed;
- only a subset of workers are newly treated;
- not all legal entitlement is used;
- employers/workers may offset leave through workload or scheduling;
- broad Life Ladder may be less responsive than stress, affect or job satisfaction;
- country-year shocks can be much larger than a one- or two-day entitlement change.

Therefore null or baseline-sensitive aggregate results do not contradict worker-level vacation-recovery literature.

## 5. Methodological position

The treatment timing is staggered and heterogeneous, so conventional TWFE event-study coefficients remain descriptive only.

Method anchors:

- Callaway & Sant'Anna (2021), *Journal of Econometrics*, DOI: 10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.12.001.
- Sun & Abraham (2021), *Journal of Econometrics*, DOI: 10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.09.006.

ARIS4C019 adds a further methodological emphasis: **legal treatment isolation must precede estimator sophistication**.

## 7. Claim boundary

The paper can claim an unusually transparent legal-event and robustness audit.

It cannot claim a robust positive or negative population causal effect of statutory annual leave from the present evidence.
