# ARIS4C019 · Literature Position v0.1

_Frozen before Pilot-0 outcome estimation: 2026-09-21._

## What is already known

The nearest causal literature is not “statutory annual leave across countries,” but **working-time reduction**.

- Lepinteur (2019), *Labour Economics*, studied reforms reducing weekly working hours in France and Portugal using difference-in-differences and reported increases in job and leisure satisfaction among affected workers. DOI: 10.1016/j.labeco.2018.05.010
- Voglino et al. (2022), *BMJ Open*, systematically reviewed reduced-working-time interventions. The small literature was concentrated in Scandinavian/workplace settings; sleep, stress and working-life quality tended to improve, while broader general-health/well-being conclusions remained uncertain. DOI: 10.1136/bmjopen-2021-051131
- A 2026 scoping review of reduced-hours-with-maintained-pay interventions similarly reports generally favorable work-life-balance and mental-health patterns but emphasizes heterogeneous interventions and geographically limited evidence. DOI: 10.5271/sjweh.4266

These studies make a positive effect plausible, but they do not establish that **increasing statutory annual-leave entitlement at population scale** raises national life evaluation.

## What ARIS4C019 is testing

019 deliberately separates a different policy margin:

> statutory paid annual-leave reforms → population Life Ladder trajectories

This differs from:
- four-day-week trials;
- employer-level reduced-hours interventions;
- cross-sectional country comparisons;
- individual self-reported vacation use;
- public-holiday counts.

The contribution is therefore defined by the **exposure/design combination**, not by claiming that leave or working time has never been studied.

## Why the answer is not predetermined

There are several mechanisms that can weaken or reverse a simple “more leave = happier” story:

- entitlement may not be fully used or enforced;
- workload may intensify before/after leave;
- reforms may affect only subsets of workers;
- macro shocks may dominate population life evaluation;
- the value of an extra day may depend on the baseline leave level;
- statutory leave may have weaker realized-time effects than reduced weekly hours.

Null and heterogeneous estimates are therefore theoretically meaningful.

## Methodological position

The treatment timing is staggered and effects may be heterogeneous.

- Callaway & Sant'Anna (2021), *Journal of Econometrics*, provide group-time ATT methods for multiple periods and variation in treatment timing. DOI: 10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.12.001
- Sun & Abraham (2021), *Journal of Econometrics*, show that conventional TWFE lead/lag event studies can be contaminated under heterogeneous treatment effects. DOI: 10.1016/j.jeconom.2020.09.006

Accordingly, ARIS4C019 does not use a conventional TWFE event-study coefficient as its headline causal estimate.

## Claim boundary

Until Pilot-0 is executed:

- the project may claim that a legally verified, outcome-covered reform panel has been assembled;
- it may claim that donor/support diagnostics pass the frozen design thresholds;
- it may **not** claim that additional leave improves or reduces happiness.
