# ARIS4C019 · 2012 global statutory-leave × Life Ladder cross-section

**Date:** 2026-09-24  
**Exposure:** ILO/TRAVAIL minimum statutory annual leave, standardized to a five-day workweek, first-year worker.  
**Outcome:** WHR2024 annual Life Ladder for 2012.

## Matched sample

- matched exact statutory-leave × Life Ladder countries: **114**
- Pearson correlation: **0.079**
- Spearman correlation: **0.085**

## Regression results

- Bivariate OLS (HC3): +5 leave days → **0.073** Life Ladder points, 95% CI [-0.100, 0.246], N=114.
- Adjusted for log GDP per capita + ILO region fixed effects (HC3): +5 leave days → **0.028**, 95% CI [-0.103, 0.160], N=114.

## Interpretation

The raw cross-country association is expected to mix leave policy with income, welfare-state development, labour institutions and region. The GDP+region model is still **cross-sectional association**, not causal identification.

The purpose of this module is to answer a different question from the modern fixed-effects panel:
- cross-section: do countries with larger statutory minima also report higher Life Ladder at one historical point?
- fixed effects: when a country's measured leave entitlement changes, does its Life Ladder move within country?

Agreement across both would be stronger evidence. Divergence is informative and suggests between-country institutional confounding.

Do not interpret this model as the effect of granting an individual worker five more vacation days.
