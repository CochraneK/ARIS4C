# ARIS4C019 · Historical statutory paid-leave spine v1.0

**Frozen:** 2026-09-24  
**Purpose:** give 019 a century-scale institutional/legal backbone instead of treating 2005–2020 as the whole history.

## Century-scale milestones

| period | historical evidence | interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Early 20th century | General statutory paid annual leave begins to emerge, initially often for public servants and selected worker groups. | Institutional origin phase. |
| **1934** | ILO 2026 historical review reports only **12 countries worldwide** with general statutory paid-annual-leave schemes covering all workers. | Pre-C052 global baseline; statutory leave was still rare. |
| **1936** | ILO Convention C052 adopted; minimum international benchmark = **6 working days** after one year of continuous service. | First international paid-holiday standard. |
| **1938–1939** | Earliest current-register C052 ratifications include Mexico and Brazil (1938), Denmark and France (1939). | Pre-war international diffusion is directly observed in NORMLEX. |
| **1952** | C101 extends the international paid-holiday framework to agriculture. | Sectoral expansion. |
| **1970** | C132 adopted; minimum benchmark becomes **at least 3 working weeks** for one year of service. | Major upward revision of international standard. |
| **1970s** | 14 of the current 39 C132 ratifiers ratify in the 1970s. | First large C132 diffusion wave. |
| **1990s** | 12 further current C132 ratifiers; ILO/NATLEX 2000 review captures 41 non-ratifying countries with recently adopted/amended general annual-leave laws. | Large post-Cold-War / legal-reform wave. |
| **2000 snapshot** | ILO/NATLEX 41-country selected frame: 25 at >=3 weeks, 15 below 3 weeks, China general law recorded with no specified minimum in that review. | Late-20th-century national-law reconstruction layer. |
| **2012 snapshot** | ILO/TRAVAIL: **155 countries/territories**, 147 exact harmonized first-year values, 7 explicit no-universal-minimum cases, 1 interval-only case. | Broadest recovered historical legal cross-section in 019. |
| **2018 survey** | ILO survey covers 124 countries; Europe had the strongest concentration above the 3-week benchmark, while Asia and the Americas had larger shares below it. | Modern global benchmark; use as contextual validation, not a replacement for national law. |
| **2026** | ILO policy review describes paid annual leave as part of a wider leave architecture and notes ongoing shifts in scope, qualifying rules and digital-era protections. | Contemporary endpoint, not part of the causal treatment series. |

## What this historical spine changes

019 is no longer a short WHR-era study. It now has four distinct temporal layers:

1. **Institutional diffusion:** 1930s–2025 via C052/C132 exact NORMLEX histories.
2. **Late-20th-century national law:** ILO/NATLEX 2000 legal snapshot plus primary-law reform reconstruction.
3. **Broad historical legal level:** ILO/TRAVAIL 2012 155-country cross-section.
4. **Outcome history:** 1870–2023 actual working hours + WDH published wellbeing trends from 1946 onward + modern annual WHR Life Ladder.

These layers must remain analytically distinct. Ratification, legal entitlement, actual hours and subjective wellbeing are related but not interchangeable variables.
