# ARIS4C019 · Great Britain 2007/2009 staged leave reform WDH event sensitivity

**Frozen event before outcome look:** statutory entitlement increased from 4 weeks to 4.8 weeks on 1 Oct 2007 and to 5.6 weeks / max 28 days on 1 Apr 2009.  
**Isolation tier:** **B** — the policy was explicitly linked to bank/public holidays and must not be interpreted as a clean annual-leave-only shock.  
**Outcome:** WDH 121C four-step verbal LifeSatisfaction, transformed to 0–10.

## Outcome design

- pre: **2004–2006**
- transition: **2007–2009** excluded from main before/after contrast
- post: **2010–2012**
- fixed equal-weight donor pool: Belgium, Denmark, France, Ireland, Italy, Luxembourg, Netherlands
- within-country-year multiple WDH findings are averaged first.

## Main descriptive result

- UK pre mean: **7.103**
- UK post mean: **7.257**
- donor pre mean: **7.190**
- donor post mean: **7.062**
- donor-adjusted before/after change: **+0.281 LifeSatisfaction points**

## Sensitivity

- 2010 only: **+0.225**
- 2010–2011: **+0.227**
- 2010–2012: **+0.281**
- pre restricted to 2004–2005: **+0.270**
- leave-one-donor-out range: **+0.217 to +0.334**

The sign is stable to dropping one donor, but the donor-placebo absolute-rank diagnostic is **0.625**; comparable Western-European series also display large changes over this crisis-era window.

## Interpretation lock

> The UK staged reform is associated with a positive donor-adjusted WDH LifeSatisfaction shift in this descriptive window, but the signal is not unusually large relative to donor placebo changes and cannot be attributed specifically to additional statutory annual leave.

Reasons not to upgrade this to a leave-only causal claim:
- the reform itself is public-holiday-linked;
- 2007–2009 overlaps the global financial crisis and other macro shocks;
- donor countries are not exchangeable randomized controls;
- the policy had two stages;
- country-average LifeSatisfaction is ecological.

Keep this as a **positive B-tier historical signal**, not as evidence that “adding 8 statutory days raises happiness by 0.28 points.”
