# ARIS4C019 · Bounded Legal-Queue Audit v0.1

**Audit date:** 2026-09-24  
**Scope:** Tajikistan 2015, Montenegro 2010/2015, Ecuador 2013 only  
**Rule:** stop after these three country investigations; do not keep expanding the queue to manufacture sample size.

## Result

This bounded audit found **no new clean stand-alone annual-leave treatment**.

### Tajikistan 2015

The World Bank panel shows a `-3` jump, but the legal evidence does not support a statutory annual-leave reduction:

- a 2015 business-law guide states that every employee had at least **24 calendar days** of annual leave;
- the Labour Code adopted in 2016 likewise sets the minimum annual basic leave at **24 calendar days**.

**Decision:** reject the EW2015 jump as a treatment. Treat it as a measurement/coding or standardized-case recoding unless a primary legal source proving an actual 2014/2015 reduction is found.

### Montenegro

The queue contains a +1 jump around EW2010 and a -0.33 jump around EW2015. Neither matches the documented statutory reform.

ILO supervisory material records that amendments published in **Official Gazette No. 59/2011 (14 December 2011)** changed section 65(1) so annual paid leave became **not less than 20 working days**. Earlier comparative material reported Montenegro at **18 working days**.

This means:

- the queue's **2010 +1** jump is not the legal event;
- the **2015 -0.33** average-profile movement is not a new statutory leave reform;
- the legally meaningful Montenegro event is the **2011 Labour Law amendment**, already represented conceptually by the existing Montenegro candidate track;
- because the change sits inside a broader Labour Law amendment, it is **not a clean leave-specific holdout**.

**Decision:** reject both queue jumps as separate treatments. Keep the 2011 reform only as a broad-labour-law candidate/sensitivity.

### Ecuador 2013

A 2012 country report describes Ecuadorian workers as entitled to a continuous **15-day annual vacation**, plus one additional day per year after five years of service. A 2014 report describes the same entitlement.

There is therefore no evidence that the small `-0.33` World Bank average movement around EW2013 reflects a statutory annual-leave reduction.

**Decision:** reject as treatment.

## Scientific consequence

The legal queue has now demonstrated why panel discontinuities cannot be promoted automatically to reforms:

- Tajikistan: apparent jump without legal change;
- Montenegro: panel timing does not align with the actual 2011 law change;
- Ecuador: small standardized-case movement without a statutory change.

The queue is therefore **closed for this Pilot-0 cycle**. Additional events should be added only through a future, separately frozen legal-search protocol, not by continuing to inspect World Bank jumps after outcome results are known.
