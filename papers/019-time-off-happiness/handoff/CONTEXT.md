# ARIS4C019 · Context

ARIS4C019 began as a question about whether countries becoming more generous with vacation/time off also become happier.

The scientifically stronger design is longitudinal and legal-event based. Static cross-country levels are heavily confounded by development, institutions, welfare-state structure, culture and labour-market composition.

The project now separates two empirical layers:

1. **Original eight-event stress-test panel** — legally verified reforms in which annual leave changed, but most are bundled with broader labour/time-off policies. It is retained for transparency and robustness analysis, not as a clean annual-leave causal panel.
2. **Independent Israel leave-specific holdout** — a cleaner Annual Leave Law amendment discovered after the original analysis through an outcome-blind legal-snapshot screen, then frozen before its Life Ladder outcome was opened.

Current evidence is neither a stable positive effect nor a clean causal zero. The eight-event mean is fragile and Bahrain-sensitive; Israel is near zero under the frozen 2015 reference but materially negative under post-outcome alternative baselines.

The resulting manuscript contribution is therefore methodological and substantive: exact legal treatment isolation, exposure-outcome alignment, source-version robustness, prospective donor rules and independent holdout logic materially change what can be claimed from cross-national policy-outcome data.

Positive/negative affect remain locked. The primary outcome for the current Pilot-0 paper is annual national Life Ladder only.
