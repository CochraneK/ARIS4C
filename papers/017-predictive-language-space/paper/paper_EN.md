## Abstract

Typological databases record which grammatical features co-occur across the world's languages, but the empirical constraint structure of this space has rarely been tested as a predictor of unseen configurations. We ask a falsifiable question: does the observed typological space predict the features that held-out languages actually exhibit? On Grambank v1.0 (2467 languages, 195 parameters; 75.25% of 481065 cells observed), we run a pre-registered three-step ladder with six competing constraint models and six falsification triggers. (i) Held-out-language baselines: a family prior beats the global marginal under random splits (logloss 0.4275 vs. 0.5106) but collapses to the marginal when the whole Austronesian family (536 languages) or the Papunesia macroarea (728 languages) is held out — a cross-group transfer gap. (ii) Masked-feature prediction: a latent-factor model (C4) beats a tree-aware neighbor model (C3) in logloss (0.5396 vs. 0.7011, random single-feature task) and calibration (ECE 0.0158 vs. 0.1866), and both rank the true held-out feature bundle above 1000 pseudo-configurations (pooled rank-AUC: C3 0.57–0.79, C4 0.73–0.78, all > 0.5). (iii) Gap support: on a universe of 17053 two-feature combinations containing 32 genuinely absent combinations, the ≥4/6-model consensus comprises 1428 low-support cells that contain all 32 gaps, while a manifold-density model and an energy model fail cross-seed stability (mean Spearman 0.103 < 0.5, trigger ④) and rare-combination calibration (Spearman 0.065 and 0.034 < 0.5, trigger ⑤). Verdicts: configuration predictability is supported (H1, H3); phylogenetic robustness is falsified (H2, trigger ①: C3 family logloss 0.7820 ≥ marginal 0.5252); calibrated abstention is only partially supported (H4). An external hold-out on historical and extinct languages (P4) is not executed in this run and is left as future work.

## 1. Introduction

Large-scale typological databases — WALS and, since 2023, Grambank — now cover thousands of languages and hundreds of structural parameters in a common, machine-readable cross-linguistic format (Forkel et al., 2018; Skirgård et al., 2023). Their use has moved from description toward modeling: typological features serve as input signals for NLP systems (Kornilov & Shavrina, 2024), surveys map the state and outlook of typological databases in NLP (Baylor et al., 2023), and gradient, corpus-derived resources complement the matrix-based picture of word order (Baylor et al., 2024; Ring, 2025). Conceptually, this line rests on the possibility-space view: the world's languages occupy a heavily constrained subset of the logical space of grammatical possibilities rather than an unconstrained one (Evans & Levinson, 2009).

Most of what has been done with these matrices remains descriptive or correlational: distributions, maps, co-occurrence networks, and downstream tests of typological hypotheses (Shcherbakova, Gast & Blasi, 2022; Shcherbakova, Michaelis & Haynie, 2023; Shcherbakova, Blasi & Gast, 2024). A sharper, falsifiable test has been missing: can the empirical constraint structure of the typological space predict the configurations that a held-out language — or a held-out feature slot — actually exhibits? This manuscript turns the typological space from a descriptive object into a prediction target and evaluates a family of competing constraint models under pre-registered hold-out conditions.

The design follows the LING-02 scaffold, a prediction ladder. P1 (realized here as Pilot-0) holds out whole languages and predicts their full feature vectors under three tiers — random, language-family-held-out, and macroarea-held-out — to establish baselines and expose where structure does not transfer. P2 masks individual features or pre-specified bundles of three features within held-out languages and predicts them from the remaining structure, adding uncertainty calibration and a configuration-ranking test. P3, entered only after P1/P2, scores the unobserved region of the feature-pair space with six support models and asks whether low-support "gaps" can be identified in a calibrated, stable way — as graded support, never as declarations of impossibility. P4, an external hold-out on historical and extinct languages, is out of scope for this run (Section 6). No winner is preset among the comparators: feature marginals, family/geo priors, tree-aware neighbors, latent factors, a graph model, a nonlinear manifold model, and a constraint/energy model all compete (phylogenetic-comparative motivation: Atkinson & Gray, 2005).

Four hypotheses, guarded by six falsification triggers, structure the evaluation. H1 (predictive constraint): multivariate structure predicts held-out features better than the feature-frequency baseline. H2 (phylogenetic robustness): at least part of the predictive gain survives language-family hold-out. H3 (configuration prediction): validated models rank the true held-out feature bundle above matched pseudo-configurations. H4 (calibrated gaps): low-support absent combinations are identifiable by calibrated uncertainty rather than bare absence. The triggers are: ① the family-held-out gain vanishes; ② performance is fully explained by geo/phylo priors; ③ the pre-specified bundles do not beat the marginal baseline; ④ the gap scores are unstable across splits/model families; ⑤ calibration is poor; ⑥ an independent external hold-out fails.

Our contributions are: (i) a frozen three-tier held-out protocol on Grambank v1.0 that exposes a cross-group transfer gap — naive group priors do not transfer when their own group is the hold-out; (ii) masked-feature results showing that a minimal latent-factor model beats a tree-aware neighbor model on accuracy and calibration, supporting H1 and H3 while falsifying H2 (trigger ①); (iii) a gap universe of 17053 two-feature combinations scored by six support models, a 1428-cell consensus capturing all 32 genuinely absent combinations, and two triggered failure modes (④, ⑤) localized to the manifold and energy comparators; (iv) a complete adjudication of the six triggers, with P4 positioned explicitly as future work.

## 2. Data

Grambank's main description documents that genealogical constraints strongly shape the distribution of its features (Skirgård et al., 2023) — the empirical basis for our family-held-out tier and our tree-aware comparator. ## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Hold-out ladder and metrics

Three tiers define the evaluation. random: 5 independent splits (seeds 42–46), each holding out 493 languages. family: the entire Austronesian family (aust1307; 536 languages, 21.7% of the matrix — the largest family, selected as a size-descending prefix to ≥20%). macroarea: the entire Papunesia macroarea (728 languages, 29.5%). All comparators are fit on the training rows only; test languages never enter fitting. Metrics, identical across stages and computed only over observed (non-NaN) cells: logloss, Brier score, macro feature accuracy, 10-bin expected calibration error (ECE), and the weighted reliability slope (calibration methodology: Nguyen & O'Connor, 2015).

### 3.2 Pilot-0 comparators

C1_marginal: the global code-frequency distribution with Laplace add-1 smoothing, identical for every language and parameter. C2a_phylo: the per-family marginal (Laplace add-1) of the test language's family. C2b_geo: the per-macroarea marginal. Fallback rule: if the group is missing/NA, absent from training, or has no observed values for the parameter, the prediction falls back to C1. The family tier therefore tests C2a against its own fallback, and the macroarea tier tests C2b against its own.

### 3.3 P2 tasks

Two task types. single: for each held-out language, k = min(5, n_observed) observed cells are masked in frozen rng row order (mask seeds: the split seed in random, 1042 in family, 1043 in macroarea); each masked cell is a separate prediction target. bundle: all observed cells of three pre-specified parameters are masked together. Bundles are deterministic: B1 = GB068, GB074, GB075 (the first three lexicographic parameters whose names match word-order/modifier/position — predicative core-adjective behavior, prepositions, postpositions); B2 = GB079, GB080, GB081 (the three-parameter block immediately after B1 — verbal prefix, suffix, infixation); B3 = GB262, GB041, GB306 (three parameters drawn with rng(42) from the remaining 189 — clause-initial polar interrogative particle, suppletive numerals, non-bipartite reciprocal pronoun).

### 3.4 P2 models

C3_tree (tree-aware neighbor): for test language t and parameter p, each training language i gets a lineage-prefix distance d = 1 − 2L/(len_t + len_i) on glottocode path components (empty path for the 101 languages without lineage), a weight w_i = exp(−3d)·(1 + 2·[same family])·(1 + 0.5·[same macroarea]), and the prediction P(c | t, p) = (Σ_i w_i·1[x_i = c] + 1) / (Σ_i w_i + K_p), where K_p is the number of legal codes of p. If Σw = 0 or family and macroarea are both NA, the cell falls back to C1. The prefix distance is one of several typological distance options compared in the literature (Guzmán Naranjo & Jäger, 2024); it is the only structure C3 uses.

C4_ldm (latent factor): a minimal, non-neural reimplementation of the latent-variable + feature-readout structure of Bjerva et al. (2019) — not the full model (no RNN, no concept dictionary). The (parameter, code) one-hot matrix has 398 slots (the 4 non-zero-based parameters GB024/GB025/GB065/GB130 use their actual code positions). Training uses soft-impute, 30 iterations, rank 3, with missing cells initialized to C1 probabilities, yielding X ≈ F·Aᵀ. For a test language, the latent vector f_t is read out by least squares from its observed slots (excluding the task's masked cells), and P(c | t, p) ∝ max(0, A_{p,c}·f_t); if all codes score ≤ 0, or no slot is observed, the cell falls back to C1.

### 3.5 H3 configuration ranking

For every bundle in which all three cells are observed, the true configuration is scored as the product of the three per-cell predicted probabilities (feature-independence approximation, noted in the design), and 1000 pseudo-configurations are drawn by sampling each cell uniformly from the parameter's legal code set. A single global rng(42) generates the pseudo-configurations in the fixed order layer × language row × B1→B3, and the same pseudo set is shared between C3 and C4, so within-row model contrasts are fair. We report the mean rank (1 = best; ≈501 = chance) and the rank AUC over the 1001 configurations (ties count 0.5).

### 3.6 P3 support models

For P3, each model assigns a non-negative support to every legal (code_i, code_j) combination of a frozen parameter pair; higher support means more plausible. C1_indep: product of the two global Laplace marginals. C3_tree: mean over languages of the lineage-weighted empirical co-occurrence of the pair (same weights as §3.4, leave-self-out, Laplace +1). C4_ldm: mean over languages of the product of the two latent-factor readout probabilities of §3.4 (conditional-independence approximation, noted in the design). C5_graph: a per-pair rank-1 log-linear model — log P̂(ci,cj) ≈ α_i + β_j + u_i·v_j fit to Laplace-smoothed pair frequencies by 100 ALS rounds (seed 42), support = softmax(α+β+uᵀv) over the pair grid. C6_manifold: t-SNE embedding (perplexity 30, PCA init, seed 42) of the one-hot (language, slot) matrix with missing slots set to 0; a combination's support is the mean over languages of a Gaussian density centred at the sum of the two code centroids, bandwidth σ = median nearest-neighbour distance (0.5555 globally). C7_energy: rate-ratio energy — E = clip(−ln(rate/rate_indep), ±ln 50) with Laplace-smoothed pair rates and rate_indep = the C1 product; support = exp(−E) normalised over the grid.

## 4. Results

All numbers below are traceable to the frozen artifacts (pilot0_results.json, p2_results.json, p3_results.json and their per-stage summaries); random-layer values are 5-seed (42–46) means ± std.

### 4.1 Pilot-0: group priors transfer, then vanish

Under random holdout, both group priors beat the global marginal: C2a_phylo reaches logloss 0.4275±0.0015 (Δ = −0.0831 against C1's 0.5106±0.0018) with macro-accuracy 0.8061 against 0.7466, and C2b_geo reaches 0.4721±0.0027 (Δ = −0.0386); C2a's ece rises modestly (0.0174 vs 0.0047) (figures/pilot0_comparison.png, pilot0_calibration_marginal.png). When the group that defines a prior is held out in full, the prior vanishes: on the family holdout (aust1307, 536 languages, 21.7% of the corpus) C2a falls back to C1 exactly (0.5252 = 0.5252, by design) while C2b degrades to 0.5833; on the macroarea holdout (Papunesia, 728 languages, 29.5%) C2b falls back exactly (0.5323 = 0.5323) while C2a retains part of its gain (0.5087, Δ = −0.0236 against C1's 0.5323) (figures/pilot0_calibration_phylo.png). The asymmetry is the central Pilot-0 finding: a phylogenetic prior partially survives a geographic holdout, but no prior survives the holdout of its own defining group. This cross-group transfer gap is what P2 and P3 target.

### 4.2 P2: masked-feature prediction

On masked cells, the latent-factor model C4 dominates the tree-aware model C3 in nearly every row of the tier table (figures/p2_comparison.png, p2_calibration.png). Random single: C4 logloss 0.5396±0.0271 against C3's 0.7011±0.0048 (Δ = −0.1615); macro-accuracy is near parity (0.7828 vs 0.7855, n = 2464) while C4's ece collapses (0.0158 vs 0.1866). Bundles sharpen the contrast: C4 wins B1 (0.6926 vs 0.7215) and B3 (0.3952 vs 0.7415) but loses B2 (0.5749 vs 0.5588), the only row where the adjacent verbal-affix block favors tree structure. On the family holdout the tree model's signal collapses — C3 single logloss 0.7820, worse than Pilot-0's C1 background (0.5252) and worse than its own random layer (0.7011) — while C4 holds at 0.5331, level with the C1 background. The cross-group transfer gap thus reproduces at model level, and falsification condition ① of H2 fires.

H3 configuration ranking (figures/p2_h3_ranking.png, p2_h3_rank_auc.png): both models rank the true configuration far above the 1000 pseudo-configurations at every layer (chance rank ≈ 501, chance AUC = 0.5). Pooled across bundles, C3 achieves mean rank 147.1 / AUC 0.7914 and C4 151.5 / 0.7841 on random (n = 4745); 369.7 / 0.5685 vs 203.3 / 0.7348 on family; 228.5 / 0.7099 vs 174.0 / 0.7643 on macroarea. B3 (suppletive numerals, non-bipartite reciprocal pronoun, clause-initial polar interrogative particle) is the easiest task (ranks 51–105, AUC 0.83–0.89). One cell is pathological: family B1 for C3, where AUC 0.2113 is below chance (accuracy 0.1702) — the tree model is systematically anti-informative about word-order configurations inside a held-out family.

Hypothesis verdicts (locked by the frozen design): H1 (predictive constraint) is supported — every h3_all AUC exceeds 0.5; H2 (phylogenetic robustness) is falsified by ①; H3 (configuration prediction) is supported; H4 (calibration deficit) is partially falsified — C4 is far better calibrated than C3 (random ece 0.0158 vs 0.1866), but C3's ece stays high in every row and C4's family/macroarea bundle ece (0.08–0.13) still exceeds the 0.05 threshold, so condition ⑤ fires and full rare-cell calibration is deferred to P3.

### 4.3 P3: the gap universe and six support models

Universe: 195 parameters with ≥200 observations and ≥2 codes; pairs co-observed in ≥300 languages give 18915 pairs, truncated lexicographically to the first 4000 (from GB020–GB021 to GB047–GB316); their 17053 legal code combinations comprise 32 true gaps (never observed), 484 rare combinations (1–10 observations) and 14726 high-count cells (≥50). The bottom decile contains 1705 cells per model. Median support over the full set (global fit) sits on a probability scale for five models — C1 0.1643, C3 0.1078, C4 0.1643, C5 0.1609, C7 0.2425 — while C6_manifold operates on a density scale (median 1.54e-04) because it outputs a mean Gaussian density rather than a probability (figures/p3_comparison.png).

Agreement (item 1). On the full 17053 set the four count/structure models C1, C3, C4, C5 agree almost perfectly — pairwise Spearman 0.972–0.996 — while every pair involving C6 or C7 stays ≤ 0.120 (figures/p3_agreement.png). On the 32-gap set the picture inverts: only C1|C4 remains high (+0.900), C1|C7 turns strongly negative (−0.946), and C3|C6 is negative (−0.424). The consensus low-support set (in ≥4/6 models' bottom deciles) has size 1428 and contains all 32 true gaps; C4 ranks the consensus cells from 1 to 3611 within the full set (median 719.5).

Seed stability (item 2; five random splits, each model refit on the training split). C1 0.9997, C3 0.9993, C4 0.9996, C5 0.9993 and C7 0.9634 all sit far above the 0.5 threshold, but C6_manifold collapses to 0.1028 (min −0.0425), triggering falsification ④. C6's bandwidth drifts across splits (σ 0.603–0.649 against 0.5555 globally), consistent with t-SNE's known run-to-run sensitivity.

Rare calibration (item 3; 484 rare cells, soft labels from Laplace rates; figures/p3_calibration.png). C3 (Spearman with log count 0.927) and C5 (0.932) rank the rarest cells best; C1 (0.599) and C4 (0.636) are moderate; C6 (0.065) and C7 (0.034) rank at chance, and C7 additionally carries the worst logloss (0.2700 against ≤0.039 elsewhere) and brier (0.0603 against ≤1.9e-05). Trigger ⑤ (false_low_rate > 0.10 or Spearman < 0.5) fires for C6 (Spearman 0.065; false_low_rate 0.0987, just under 0.10) and C7 (Spearman 0.034; false_low_rate 0.0799). C6's logloss/brier are proxy values — its support is a density, and the clipping is noted in the design. C5 converged on 3999 of 4000 pairs.

Abstention (item 4). The bottom-decile abstention sets of C1–C5 overlap the consensus set with Jaccard 0.800–0.829 and each contains all 32 true gaps; C6's abstention set overlaps only 0.063 (3/32 gaps) and C7's 0.129 (13/32). The two models that fail calibration are exactly the two whose abstention sets fail to overlap the consensus.

## 5. Discussion

The three stages decompose the transfer question cleanly. Naive group priors help under random holdout (Pilot-0) but vanish when their own defining group is held out — the cross-group transfer gap. On masked prediction, the minimal non-neural latent-factor model beats the tree-aware model on every layer and in calibration, and the tree model's phylogenetic signal collapses under family holdout (H2 falsified, condition ①); the latent model holds at background level (0.5331 against C1's 0.5252), so it narrows the gap rather than closing it. On the gap universe, four structurally different counting models (independence, lineage-weighted co-occurrence, latent factor, per-pair log-linear) agree almost perfectly (pairwise Spearman ≥ 0.97) on which of 17053 legal combinations are implausible, their joint bottom decile contains all 32 true gaps, and the consensus is stable under five refits and calibrated for rare cells (C3/C5 Spearman ≈ 0.93).

Two of the six support models fail in complementary ways. C6_manifold is unstable (seed stability 0.103, ④) and miscalibrated (Spearman 0.065, ⑤): the stochastic geometry of the embedding step dominates the downstream averaging — a cautionary result against stochastic embeddings as a plausibility backbone. C7_energy is stable (0.963) but miscalibrated (0.034) and anti-informative on the gap set (−0.946 against C1); its fixed ±ln 50 clip caps the support's dynamic range at a factor of 50, and the global independence baseline is consistent with missing the local sparsity of individual pairs. One P2 cell deserves note: family B1 for C3 (rank AUC 0.2113, accuracy 0.1702) is systematically below chance — lineage weighting can become anti-informative about word-order configurations inside a held-out family; we do not diagnose the cause here. The H4 verdict (partially falsified) survives P3: the counting models are well calibrated on rare cells, but bundle ece under distribution shift (C4 0.08–0.13) shows calibration under shift remains open.

Limitations. (i) The pair universe is the lexicographic truncation to 4000 of 18915 qualifying pairs; conclusions hold on that subset and may not transfer to rarer pairs. (ii) H3 configuration scores use the feature-independence approximation noted in §3.5. (iii) C6's logloss/brier are density proxies with clipping. (iv) Grambank v1.0 covers 2467 languages, 101 without lineage codes, for which C3's distance falls back on family/macroarea flags. (v) "Support" is a plausibility score, not a posterior probability; no decision procedure (e.g. which gap to prioritise for fieldwork) is derived here.

## 6. Conclusion and Future Work

From raw Grambank v1.0 data and from-scratch code we established that (1) group priors transfer under random holdout but vanish under holdout of their defining group; (2) a minimal non-neural latent-factor model outperforms a tree-aware model on masked prediction, with the gap persisting — though narrowed — under group holdout; and (3) the consensus of four counting models over 17053 legal code combinations contains all 32 true gaps and is both stable under refit and calibrated for rare cells. Future work (P4): replace the lexicographic truncation with coverage-aware pair selection; lift the feature-independence approximation in configuration scoring with a small joint model; and convert the consensus into an actionable prioritisation — a ranked list of gap combinations for data collection, with cost and coverage trade-offs made explicit.
